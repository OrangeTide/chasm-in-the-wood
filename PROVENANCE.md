# Provenance

This file records where the project's shipped art and type assets come from, and
the terms under which they are included. The game code itself is released under the
Unlicense (see `UNLICENSE.txt`). The assets below carry their own terms, recorded
here so the grant is auditable rather than only described in prose.

For the policy that governs what art the project accepts, see the Art policy section
of `docs/build-and-assets.md`.

## Font: Solid Frame (baked as `content/fonts/ui.cfn`)

- Tracked file: `content/fonts/ui.cfn`, a CFN bitmap font.
- Compiled-in fallback: the same face is baked into `src/gui/guigl.pas` as the
  default when no CFN file loads.
- Source: the Solid Frame TTF, baked to a bitmap at pixel size 9 across printable
  ASCII (32..126). The baking process is described in `content/fonts/README.md`.
- Author: potatotastegood, https://potatotastegood.itch.io/solid-frame
- What ships: only the baked bitmap. The source `.ttf` is kept out of the
  repository. A 9-pixel ASCII bitmap does not expose the TTF for extraction, so the
  licence terms below permit it as embedded data in a finished project.
- Licence: a custom licence that ships with the font download. Its terms, quoted:
  - You can use this font in commercial and non-commercial projects.
  - You can't edit, resell, redistribute or share the font file separately.
  - Redistribution is allowed only when the font is embedded as part of a finished
    project that does not expose the font for extraction.
  - Credit is not required, but greatly appreciated.
- Note: the licence forbids redistributing the font file itself, so the `.ttf` is
  not committed. Only the baked `ui.cfn` bitmap ships, as embedded data in the
  finished game. Attribution is given above though the licence does not require it.

## Player character (`content/models/player.vox`)

- Tracked file: `content/models/player.vox`, a MagicaVoxel `.vox` model loaded as the
  player at runtime. When it is absent the game falls back to the placeholder ball.
- Source: generated by Jon with VoxelAI (https://voxelai.ai) as a GLB, then brought to
  the game's voxel scale through `assimp` (GLB to OBJ, keeping the material diffuse
  colours) and the `voxelize` tool (OBJ to `.vox`) at the object voxel-unit. The result
  is 13x6x27 voxels, about 27 tall, in 29 colours.
- Terms: production art from a purpose-built generative voxel tool, per the Art policy in
  `docs/build-and-assets.md`.
- Licence: owned by Jon with full commercial rights, including redistribution. VoxelAI
  grants its paying subscribers full commercial rights to the models they create, and the
  model was made under a paid subscription. (Free-tier VoxelAI models are instead public
  and may be featured in the gallery; that does not apply here.)

## Monster models (`content/models/{skeleton,ooze,bat,demon,rat,spider}.vox`)

- Tracked files: MagicaVoxel `.vox` models loaded per monster at runtime, keyed by the
  lower-cased monster content id (monsters.txt). When one is absent the game falls back to
  the drawn placeholder, so the rest of the bestiary still renders.
  - From the Quaternius "Monster Pack" (lowpoly-animated-monsters): `skeleton.vox` from
    Skeleton; `ooze.vox` from Slime, the grey ooze; `bat.vox` from Bat; `demon.vox` from
    Dragon, the demon lord. One flat-shaded mesh per creature, each its own material colours.
  - From the Quaternius "Easy Animated Enemy Pack" (animated-easy-enemies): `rat.vox` from
    Rat; `spider.vox` from Spider.
- Source and pipeline: each mesh is brought to the game's voxel scale through `assimp`
  (FBX to OBJ, keeping the material diffuse colours) and the `voxelize` tool, at the kit's
  calibrated scale (monsters kit anchored on the 1.8 m skeleton; enemies kit chosen to read
  beside the hero, the kit not being internally to scale). The easy-enemy OBJ sources are
  used directly because that pack's 2019 FBX predates assimp support. Staging and conversion
  are in `tools/stage.txt` and `tools/assets.txt`; see `docs/asset-pipeline.md`.
- Author: Quaternius, https://quaternius.com (also https://quaternius.itch.io).
- Terms: CC0 (public domain dedication). Quaternius releases these packs CC0, which
  requires no attribution; the author is recorded here to keep the provenance verifiable.

## Town tiles (`content/tiles/town/{floor,wall,post,door,doorleaf}.vox`)

- Tracked files: MagicaVoxel `.vox` tiles loaded for the town environment, one per tile kind.
  When a kind is absent the game falls back to a generated placeholder, so tiles drop in one
  at a time.
  - `floor.vox` from Floor_Brick (the cobbled plaza); `wall.vox` from Wall_Plaster_Straight
    (the plastered building wall); `post.vox` from Corner_Exterior_Wood (the timber corner);
    `door.vox` from DoorFrame_Flat_WoodDark (the open doorway); `doorleaf.vox` from
    Door_1_Flat (the locked-door panel).
- Source: the Quaternius "Medieval Village MegaKit" (Standard OBJ export, in metres). Each
  mesh is voxelized at the village-kit scale (1.0, the export already being metric); the
  floor and wall are cropped to one 1.5 m cell. The kit is texture-atlas (its materials name
  a `map_Kd` base-colour image rather than a flat diffuse colour), so the voxelizer samples
  each piece's texture for its real colours. Staging and conversion are in `tools/stage.txt`
  and `tools/assets.txt`; see `docs/asset-pipeline.md`.
- Author: Quaternius, https://quaternius.com (also https://quaternius.itch.io).
- Terms: CC0 (public domain dedication). Quaternius releases these packs CC0, which requires
  no attribution; the author is recorded here to keep the provenance verifiable. The staged
  base-colour textures are part of the same CC0 kit and are used only to colour the voxels;
  they are not themselves redistributed in the game.

## Forest tiles (`content/tiles/forest/{floor,wall,post}.vox`)

- Tracked files: MagicaVoxel `.vox` tiles loaded for the forest environment. Absent kinds
  fall back to a generated placeholder.
  - `floor.vox` from RockPath_Square_Wide (the rock-path clearing floor); `wall.vox` from
    Rock_Medium_1 (a rock outcrop, extruded through the cell as a rocky wall face);
    `post.vox` from Rock_Medium_2 (a boulder at corners).
- Source: the Quaternius "Stylized Nature MegaKit" (Standard OBJ export, in metres), at the
  nature-kit scale (1.0). The kit is texture-atlas, so the voxelizer samples each piece's
  `map_Kd` base-colour image for its colours. The forest is a rocky clearing because the
  engine's edge-panel tile model cannot draw whole trees. Staging and conversion are in
  `tools/stage.txt` and `tools/assets.txt`; see `docs/asset-pipeline.md`.
- Author: Quaternius, https://quaternius.com (also https://quaternius.itch.io).
- Terms: CC0 (public domain dedication), as with the other Quaternius kits. The staged
  base-colour textures are part of the same CC0 kit and only colour the voxels; they are not
  themselves redistributed in the game.

## Humanoid foe models (`content/models/{bandit,cultist,acolyte}.vox`)

- Tracked files: MagicaVoxel `.vox` models for the human foes, loaded per monster by content
  id. Absent ones fall back to the drawn placeholder.
  - `bandit.vox` from the Male Ranger outfit; `cultist.vox` from the Male Peasant; `acolyte.vox`
    from the Female Ranger.
- Source: the Quaternius "Modular Character Outfits - Fantasy" (Godot/Unreal glTF export). Each
  outfit ships rigged in its bind (T) pose with no animation. The `pose` tool skins it at an
  Idle pose, retargeted by joint name from the Quaternius "Universal Animation Library" (UAL1,
  shared rig), and writes a posed OBJ; the voxelizer then samples the outfit's base-colour
  textures. So the figures voxelize standing naturally rather than as a T-posed scarecrow.
  Staging and conversion are in `tools/stage.txt` and `tools/assets.txt` (the `pose` lines);
  the tool chain is below. The human-kit scale is 1.0 (the glTF is metric and Y-up).
- Author: Quaternius, https://quaternius.com (also https://quaternius.itch.io).
- Terms: CC0 (public domain dedication) for both the outfit kit and the animation library, as
  with the other Quaternius kits. The staged base-colour textures are part of the same CC0
  kits and only colour the voxels; they are not themselves redistributed in the game.

## Beast foe model (`content/models/wolf.vox`)

- Tracked file: the dire wolf's `.vox`, loaded per monster by content id; absent, it falls
  back to the drawn placeholder.
- Source: the Quaternius "Farm Animals Animated" pack. Unlike the human outfits, these are
  self-animated creature rigs, each glb carrying its own Idle clip, so the `pose` tool uses
  the model's own animation (no UAL). The dog (`Pug`) serves as the dire wolf; assimp
  converts the FBX to glb, `pose` skins its Idle, and the voxelizer takes the flat material
  colours (the pack is untextured). The creature-kit scale (0.00381) sizes it to about 1.3 m
  long. Staging and the pose line are in `tools/stage.txt` and `tools/assets.txt`.
- Author: Quaternius, https://quaternius.com (also https://quaternius.itch.io).
- Terms: CC0 (public domain dedication), as with the other Quaternius kits.

## Vendored tool libraries (`third_party/`, `tools/model.*`, `tools/anim.*`)

These are build-time pieces of the asset pipeline, not shipped in the game binary. They let
`tools/gltf2glb` pack a glTF into a self-contained `.glb` and `tools/pose` skin a rigged
character at an animation pose (see `docs/asset-pipeline.md`).

- `third_party/cgltf.h`, `third_party/cgltf_write.h`: the cgltf glTF 2.0 reader and writer, by
  Johannes Kuhlmann and contributors, https://github.com/jkuhlmann/cgltf. Licence: MIT (notice
  preserved at the foot of `cgltf.h`).
- `third_party/HandmadeMath.h`: the HandmadeMath single-header math library,
  https://github.com/HandmadeMath/HandmadeMath. Licence: public domain dedication (CC0), with a
  perpetual permissive licence where that is not recognised (notice preserved in the file).
- `tools/model.{c,h}`, `tools/anim.{c,h}`: the glTF model loader and the clip-retargeting
  animator, taken from the author's own aldeby engine, which already drives these Quaternius
  rigs. The author owns this code; it is reused here under the same terms as the rest of the
  project (see `UNLICENSE.txt`).

## Not included

- Music: no tracks ship. The player supplies their own licensed `.ogg` files. See
  `content/music/README.md`.
- Reference asset packs under `REF/` are local reference material only. They are
  excluded by a global git ignore and are never committed or shipped. Many carry
  commercial licences and are not part of the distributed project.
