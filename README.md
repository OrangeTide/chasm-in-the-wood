# Chasm in the Wood

A rogue-like game.

## Introduction

The hamlet of Brackenridge sits where the old road gives up and the forest takes over. Its houses huddle beneath steep roofs, its market is small, and its people know better than to wander far beneath the trees after dusk. Still, the woods provide what the hamlet needs: timber, game, and the occasional traveler willing to pay for a warm meal and a dry bed.

Lately, the forest has been giving up stranger things.

A silver coin stamped with the face of a forgotten king. A bronze clasp green with age. A shard of blue glass no village smith can identify. Each was found near the same stretch of woodland, where the ground has begun to sink and the roots of the oldest trees twist around empty air.

You arrive in Brackenridge with little more than your gear, your ambition, and whatever brought you to the edge of the forest. By the time you reach the inn, everyone has heard the rumor: somewhere among the trees, an opening has appeared in the earth. A fissure, some say. A cave, others insist. The oldest villagers call it a bad omen and refuse to speak of it at all.

But people have already gone looking.

One returned with a handful of strange coins—and vanished from the hamlet a day later. Another came back pale and shaking, claiming that the darkness below was not empty. No one has seen the third.

The hamlet’s reeve will pay for anything useful recovered from the woods. The traders will buy anything that looks old enough to be valuable. The innkeeper will sell you provisions, though never on credit. And if you return with stories of wonders below, Brackenridge will listen—until the next descent calls you back.

The entrance lies beneath a tangle of roots, half-hidden by moss and fallen leaves. Cold air breathes from the darkness. Far below, water drips with the steady patience of a clock. Somewhere beneath the forest is a world no one remembers: buried passages, forgotten treasures, and, if the rumors are true, a city from an age lost to history.

There is no map. No promise of safe passage. Only the dark below, the hamlet behind you, and the choice to take the first step.

**The Chasm in the Wood is open.**
